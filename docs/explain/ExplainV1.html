How Grown grows its branches

The bigger egg dispenser's Grown prototype doesn't model its Lattice by hand, and it doesn't use a build123d feature. A Python script in src/grown.py draws a random cell pattern on the cage's skin, edits it until it prints, and then melts every branch into one smooth solid.

Is this build123d? No. Nothing in the project imports build123d. Branches are stored as line segments, turned into a signed distance field with numpy, meshed with scikit-image's marching cubes, and finished with manifold3d. ADR 0004 records why: build123d's exact solids can't fillet hundreds of crossing tubes into smooth Nodes. The cost is no STEP file: the cage only ever exists as a mesh.

STAGE 1 · Tube

A skin to grow on

Before any branch exists, the script defines the cage's outer surface. The class Tube describes it with two coordinates: z, the distance from the back face, and s, how far around the cross-section you are, from 0 at the bottom middle up the right side to 0.5 at the top middle and back down the left.

At every z the cross-section is a smooth spline through nine control points: the shelf edge, a lobe beside each Level's eggs, a waist pinched in between them, a shoulder, and a domed top. The points move with z, so the sides bulge 4 mm at mid-length, the top arches 6 mm, and the back and front corners round off.

STAGE 2 · Voronoi

Unroll the skin and draw cells on it

Picture slitting the Tube along its bottom middle and flattening it into a rectangle: z across, distance around the skin up the side. wrapped_voronoi scatters random seed points on that rectangle, at least 38 mm apart, and builds a Voronoi diagram with scipy: each cell is the region closer to one seed than to any other. The cell edges become Struts and the cell corners become Nodes. That is where the Original's round-celled, organic look comes from.

Two tricks make it fit a closed cage. Seeds are copied one perimeter above and below before the diagram is built, so the cells match up where the flat sheet wraps back on itself. And z is squashed by 1.25 first, then stretched back, so cells come out a little longer than they are wide.

The fixed parts are added before this, at exact positions: the Rails and Side guides from layout.py, frame lines along the shelf edges, waists and shoulders, rings at the back and front, the Exit cradle loop and V ties across the bottom.

STAGE 3 · Rules

Edit the pattern until it prints

A raw Voronoi diagram has Struts at every angle. Each half prints standing on end (z vertical), so a Strut lying too flat would droop. Several passes fix that, shown in steps 2 to 4 above:

  1. make_printable slides Nodes along z, never around, until every Strut passes ok_angle: within 45° of vertical; or within 56° if it is 24 mm or shorter (ADR 0005, to keep the cells round); or a flat bridge of 18 mm or less.
  2. route_to_bosses pushes Nodes 8 mm off the Seam. Every Strut that crossed it is replaced by two Struts that run into the nearest of the four Pin bosses, so only the bosses, Rails and Side guides cross the joint.
  3. Struts are dropped if they are still too flat, shorter than 3 mm, or inside the loading opening and finger holes at the front.
  4. prop_and_prop walks up the print from the bed. Any Node with nothing printable below it gets a new Strut down to a nearby Node that has one; if none fits, its Struts go.
  5. Dead ends are pruned, and any Strut whose curved version would cross an egg's path is dropped.
STAGE 4 · Struts

Bend and taper each branch

A straight edge looks mechanical, so strut_path bows each one sideways along the skin: a quadratic Bézier curve whose middle is pushed out by a random amount up to 18% of the Strut's length. The curve is computed in the flat (z, s) sheet and mapped back onto the Tube, so it hugs the cage. If the curved version leans too far, hits an egg's path or crosses the Seam, it retries with half the bow, then with none.

Graph.bone then gives it a bone-like taper: 2.8 mm radius at both ends, 1.9 mm in the middle, following r(t) = 1.9 + 0.9·(2t − 1)². Thick ends make the Nodes strong; the thin middle keeps print time down.

The whole skeleton

Put together, seed 1's graph is 220 chains of short tapered segments. This is everything the next stage melts into a solid. Drag to turn it.

STAGE 5 · Field

Melt the branches into one solid

This is the step build123d can't do. Instead of solid tubes joined with fillets, every segment is a distance function: for any point in space, how far it is from the segment's surface (negative inside). Tapered segments use Inigo Quilez's "round cone" formula.

The usual way to combine shapes is to take the smallest distance, which gives a hard crease where two tubes meet. capsule_field uses a smooth minimum instead:

f(p) = −k · ln Σi exp(−di(p) / k)    with k = BLEND_K = 1.8 mm

Near one Strut this is just its own distance. Where several meet, their terms add up and pull the surface outward, by up to k·ln n (about 2 mm where three Struts meet). That swelling is the webbed Node: no Node spheres are added (NODE_R = 0). Segments of the same Strut are combined with a plain minimum first, so a curved Strut doesn't bulge at its own bends.

STAGE 6 · Mesh

From field to printable halves

half_mesh runs once per half:

  1. Sample the field on a 0.4 mm grid over that half's box: about 254 × 400 × 490 values, roughly 50 million.
  2. Cut it flat with three planes by taking the maximum with each: the shelf (y ≥ 0), the bed, and the Seam. That is how the Pin bosses, Rails and Side guides get matching flat faces where the halves meet.
  3. Run marching cubes (scikit-image) to find the f = 0 surface as triangles.
  4. Drop 75% of the triangles with fast_simplification, which keeps the shape.
  5. Load it into manifold3d, confirm it is watertight, and subtract the exact D-shaped Pin holes from joint.py. The Fit coupon uses the same joint code, so it tests what the halves get.

The other prototype, Stretch (src/stretch.py), grows nothing. It loads the Original's mesh, stretches its straight middle to 16 eggs long, bends the Levels back to their Slope, and adds Side guides and V ties. Its branches are the Original designer's, just longer. It also had to be a mesh, since the Original has no B-rep.
