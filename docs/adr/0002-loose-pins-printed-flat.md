# The halves join with loose Pins printed flat, at 14 Contacts, with a lead-in press fit

Each half prints standing up, so a peg built into a half would stand vertically with its layers stacked across it, and would snap at a layer line. Instead four Pin bosses carry holes and four separate D-shaped Pins (6 x 20 mm) print lying on their flat face, with layers running along their length. Lifting a loaded Dispenser by one end puts about 20 N on the Pins against several hundred newtons each in shear. No glue, so the halves pull apart for washing, and a broken Pin is a reprint of a Pin, not of a half.

The fit copies tea-bags-drawer-divider v3's T-pad clip, also PLA Matte on this printer, which grips at 0.05 mm per side (0.1 was loose in v1) and opens to 0.1 mm per side at its ends so it starts on easily. Each hole has a chamfered mouth, a 4 mm lead-in at 0.10 mm per side that lines the halves up, then 0.05 mm per side to the bottom, so the last part takes a firm press and holds. The Fit coupon brackets the grip at 0.03, 0.05 and 0.08 mm before any half is printed.

In Grown, only things that run straight through the Seam cross it: the four Rails, four Side guides and two waist lines, plus the four Pin bosses; every Lattice Strut that would have crossed it runs into the nearest Pin boss instead. That leaves 14 small Contacts rather than a cut line round the whole cage. The user first asked for 8; letting the Side guides and waist lines run through keeps them unbroken (no gap for an egg's end to catch on), and four Pins, one per corner, are needed so the top of the Seam can't hinge open.

## Considered Options

- **Sliding dovetail:** the 4.3 mm Rails and 5 mm Struts are too thin to carry one.
- **Glue:** strongest, but the Dispenser could no longer come apart to wash.
- **A ring round the cage's outline (the first Grown):** a large flat face, but it showed as a line all the way round.
- **Two Pins only (10 Contacts):** the top of the Seam would hinge open when the Dispenser is lifted by one end.
